What We Teach

The six things we work on, week after week. None of it is rushed and none of it is drilled. It is introduced in small groups, revisited in play, and built up over terms rather than weeks.

Daily English

English is not a lesson here. It is the day.

Most children arriving at Little Lilies speak Setswana at home, and that is exactly as it should be. What we add is a full day of English around them, not an hour of it on a Tuesday.

Teachers narrate what is happening, name what children are holding, ask questions that need more than a yes, and read aloud every day. Children pick up a language by needing it and using it, so we spend the day giving them reasons to use it: asking for the blue one, telling the class what they built, retelling the end of the story.

By the year before primary school, the aim is simple. A child should walk into a Standard One classroom, understand what the teacher is saying, and be able to answer.

How Children Learn Here

A three-year-old does not learn by being told. They learn by handling something, getting it wrong, and being asked a good question about it. Our job is to set up the room so that keeps happening, and to be next to them when it does.

  • check
    We teach in small groups. New ideas are introduced to a few children at a time, so nobody hides at the back and nobody is left behind.
  • check
    We repeat, on purpose. The same rhythm, the same routines and the same concepts revisited until they are genuinely known rather than recently heard.
  • check
    We watch, and we tell you. Teachers notice what a child can and cannot yet do, and you hear about it from us early rather than at the end of the year.
