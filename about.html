Life at Little Lilies

A preschool in Mogoditshane for children two and a half to five years. Structured mornings, English all day, and meals cooked on site.

Who We Are

A small preschool in Mogoditshane, built around the way young children actually learn.

child_care

Two and a half to five

Little Lilies takes children from the first weeks of settling into a routine right through to the year before primary school. We are on Plot 17051 in Kgosing Ward, a few minutes from the Mogoditshane main road.

schedule

A deliberately structured day

Teacher-led learning happens in small groups, in short stretches that hold a young child’s attention. The rest of the day is purposeful play, the kind that is set up for a reason and watched over by an adult.

restaurant

Food already included

Meals are cooked on site and are part of the term fee. Nothing about the food is an extra charge, and allergies are catered for as a matter of course.

English runs through all of it

Not a lesson on a timetable. Stories, songs, vocabulary and conversation all day long, inside a Botswana-aligned early learning programme, so that children pick the language up by needing it and using it.

How We Teach

They are not separate subjects. They are the shape of the day itself.

groups

Guided learning in small groups

Letters, numbers, shapes and colours introduced by a teacher to a handful of children at a time. Short enough to hold attention, built up week by week rather than rushed.

toys

Purposeful play

Play that has been set up for a reason, sorting, building, water, sand, dressing up and drawing, with an adult nearby who knows what each activity is teaching.

record_voice_over

Daily English

Stories, songs, new words and a great deal of talking. Children hear English all day and are given constant, unhurried chances to use it themselves.

The Shape of a Day

The order never changes, and that is the point.

repeat

Why the same order every day

Small children settle when they know what comes next. Our day runs in the same order every day, so that by the second week a child can tell you what is about to happen, and a child who knows what is about to happen is a child who is free to concentrate.

Settling In

Starting somewhere new is a big thing for a small person, and every child does it differently. Some settle in days. Others take a few weeks. Both are completely normal, and neither says anything about your child.

  • check_circleWe usually suggest a short first day, built up over the first week.
  • check_circleSay a warm, brief goodbye rather than slipping away. It is harder in the moment and much easier by the third day.
  • check_circleCall during the morning if you want to know how your child is doing. Parents do, and we do not mind at all.
  • check_circleTell us what settles your child, a comfort item, a nickname, a routine at home, before the first day rather than after it.

How We Handle Hard Moments

Young children are still learning how to share, how to wait and how to manage feelings that are far bigger than they are. When it goes wrong, and it does, in every classroom in the world, we redirect, we name the feeling, and we help the child find the words.

We do not use physical punishment or shaming of any kind. If something in your child’s behaviour is worrying us, you will hear about it early and quietly, so that we can work on it together rather than hand you a problem at the end of term.

If something is wrong

Please tell us early. Start with your child’s teacher, who can settle most things quickly. If that does not resolve it, ask to speak to the head or the school administrator. We will acknowledge your concern promptly and tell you what we are doing about it.
