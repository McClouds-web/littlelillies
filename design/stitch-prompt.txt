GOOGLE STITCH — LITTLE LILIES PRE-SCHOOL WEBSITE
Paste this whole block as the project prompt. Generate web (desktop) first,
then ask for the mobile variants of the same screens.
================================================================================

## WHO THIS IS FOR

Little Lilies Pre-School — a new, small preschool in Mogoditshane, Botswana,
taking children from two and a half to five years old. It has no track record
yet, so the site's whole job is to make a parent trust it enough to come and
visit in person. Fees are modest and the school competes on care, structure and
honesty, not on facilities.

The visitor is a Motswana parent, mostly on an Android phone, often on mobile
data. They are asking: is my child safe here, will they learn, can I afford it,
and can I see it for myself?

## THE ONE THING THE DESIGN MUST DO

Every screen ends in the same action: **book a school visit**. Not "learn more",
not "sign up". The parent must always be one tap from arranging to walk in.

## STRUCTURE — THIS IS THE IMPORTANT PART

Do NOT lay this out as a standard marketing site. Build it as **a guided walk
through one day at the school**, from arrival to collection. Scrolling down the
homepage should feel like moving through the day, not like reading a brochure.

The homepage is a single continuous vertical journey with these moments:

  1. ARRIVAL — the front gate. A parent hands over a child. Establish warmth and
     safety immediately. The school's name and one sentence. One action.
  2. THE MORNING — circle time, songs, stories, English conversation.
  3. LEARNING — guided activities in small groups, early concepts.
  4. OUTSIDE — play.
  5. LUNCH — food is included in the fee; this matters to parents.
  6. REST — quiet time.
  7. AFTERNOON — creative activities, purposeful play.
  8. COLLECTION — the day closes, and the parent is invited to come and see it
     for themselves.

Each moment is a full-bleed horizontal band. Photograph on one side, a short
piece of text on the other, and the side alternates as you descend so the page
zig-zags. Between the bands, use a thin ribbon or path graphic that visually
connects one moment to the next, so the page reads as a single continuous route
rather than stacked sections.

Practical information — fees, age groups, downloads, contact — is NOT scattered
through the journey. It sits together at the end, after the walk, as a calm
practical block. The emotional case first, the paperwork second.

## PATTERNS TO AVOID — DO NOT PRODUCE THESE

These are the clichés that make preschool and small-business sites look alike.
Avoid all of them:

  - A hero with a small uppercase label, a big two-line headline, a paragraph
    and two side-by-side buttons.
  - A horizontal strip of three or four short value propositions under the hero.
  - A grid of feature cards with an icon, a bold title and two lines of text.
  - A numbered list of "why choose us" reasons.
  - A single full-width dark band in the brand colour, used once, as a feature.
  - A contact form sitting beside a column of contact details.
  - A three-column dark footer with logo, link list and address.
  - Rounded pill buttons and big soft drop shadows on white cards.
  - Stock-photo children who are obviously not from this school.

## VISUAL DIRECTION

Warm, human, editorial. Photography does the heavy lifting; the interface gets
out of its way. Think of a well-made children's picture book rather than a
corporate template — generous space, confident typography, one clear thing to
look at per screen.

Photographs should be large and uncropped, treated with soft organic masks
derived from the lotus petal in the school's logo, never plain rectangles and
never circles.

Text sits directly on the page, not inside boxes. Where a container is needed,
use a single hairline rule or a soft tint, not a card with a shadow.

## BRAND — USE THESE EXACT VALUES

  Deep purple    #421163   headings, primary ink
  Light purple   #865FB3   the lotus mark, accents, secondary text
  Green          #6FAB22   every call to action, without exception
  Blue           #3060A7   informational highlights only, used sparingly
  Lilac wash     #F3EEF9   section grounds
  Page           #FFFFFF

Typeface: the brand uses Century Gothic. Use Poppins, or another geometric sans
with a single-storey lowercase 'a', for headings and interface. Use a plain,
highly readable sans for body text.

The logo is a line-drawn lotus flower with three petals above a wordmark reading
"Little Lilies" over a bar reading "PRE-SCHOOL", with the strapline
"Little Steps, Big Dreams".

Green is the action colour. Purple never sits on a button.

The six things the school offers, in its own words:

    play, so children always know what comes next

    through the day

    around

## DO NOT WRITE THESE CLAIMS

The school cannot yet evidence them: "best preschool", "world class",
"full English medium", "government approved", "limited spaces". Do not state
opening hours, class sizes, staff ratios or teacher names — these are not yet
confirmed. Leave clearly marked space for them.

## SCREENS TO GENERATE

  1. Homepage — the full day-journey described above
  2. About the school — its purpose and its people
  3. Programmes — how the 2½ to 5 range is grouped
  4. Admissions and fees — the fee table, how to apply, downloadable forms
  5. Gallery — real photographs of the rooms, the food and the play area
  6. Contact and visit — map, directions, and booking a visit
  7. Mobile version of the homepage — this is how most parents will see it

================================================================================
VARIATIONS WORTH TRYING IF THE FIRST RESULT IS TOO CONVENTIONAL
================================================================================

If Stitch still returns something template-like, re-run the homepage prompt with
ONE of these replacing the day-journey structure:

  A. THE TOUR — the page is organised spatially instead of by time. The visitor
     moves room by room through the building: gate, classroom, play area,
     kitchen, bathroom, office. Each room answers the question a parent has
     about that room.

  B. THE PARENT'S QUESTIONS — the entire page is the questions parents actually
     ask, in their own words, each answered plainly and each ending in an
     invitation to come and check the answer in person.

  C. THE LETTER — the page is written as a single letter from the school to a
     parent considering it, set as one long readable column with photographs
     breaking it up. Radical, very few sites do it, and it suits a small school
     with nothing to hide.
